How this project works

This is a smart home controller. One HYBRID-X1 board does three jobs: the Arduino part reads sensors and switches things on and off, the ESP32 part talks to your phone and the weather, and the Raspberry Pi part makes the decisions and runs the dashboard.

Who does what

Arduino: the hands and sensesMotion sensors (PIR) in each room, gas, smoke and water-leak sensors, the door sensor and doorbell. It switches lights, fans, the door lock and the water and gas valves through relays.
ESP32: the messengerSends push notifications and camera snapshots to your phone, and brings in outside weather. It also lets you control the house from the phone over WiFi.
Raspberry Pi: the brainReceives every sensor event, applies the automation rules and scenes, understands voice or typed commands, and keeps the energy records.

What happens when you walk into a dark room

  1. The PIR sensor notices movement. The Arduino tells the Pi.
  2. The Pi checks the rules: it is night, and the "night motion light" rule is on.
  3. The Pi sends a command to the Arduino, which dims the light up to 60%.
  4. After 20 minutes without movement, the Pi tells the Arduino to switch it off.

Scenes and voice commands

A scene sets many things at once. Movie dims the living room and turns the rest off. Sleep turns everything off and locks the door. Away locks up, switches off the lights and arms the alarm. The voice box understands simple sentences like "set bedroom light to 30" or "lock the door". A real version would add a microphone and speech recognition on the Pi.

How the energy numbers are worked out

The Pi adds up the power of everything that is on: a basic 20 W for the electronics, up to 12 W per room light depending on brightness, and 45 W per fan. It multiplies power by time to get kWh, then multiplies by an assumed price of ₹8 per kWh. Change the price in the code to match your electricity bill.

Parts you need

HYBRID-X1 board (or Arduino Uno, ESP32 and Raspberry Pi 4), 3 PIR motion sensors, a relay board for lights and fans, an MQ-2 gas sensor, a smoke sensor, a water-leak sensor, a motorised water valve and a gas valve, a door lock solenoid with a reed switch, a doorbell button and a Pi camera.

This page is a simulation that runs in your browser, so nothing here controls a real house. Switching mains-voltage lights and fans must be done by a qualified electrician. Use certified gas and smoke detectors for real safety. HYBRID-X1 should only add alerts and automatic actions on top of them, never replace them.
