How this project works


This is a smart irrigation system for a small farm. One HYBRID-X1 board does three jobs: the Arduino part senses and switches, the ESP32 part connects wirelessly, and the Raspberry Pi part thinks and shows the dashboard. Together they water each crop only when it needs it.


Step by step




1. Sense (Arduino)Every second it reads the soil moisture of zones 1, 2 and 3, the water level in the tank and the air temperature.


2. Detect rain (ESP32)The rain sensor is read every 5 seconds. The ESP32 sends "raining" or "dry" to the Pi over WiFi.


3. Check the weather (Pi)Every 30 minutes the Pi asks a free weather service for the chance of rain in the next 6 hours.


4. Decide (Pi)The Pi compares each zone's moisture with its crop target and applies the rules below.


5. Switch (Arduino)The Pi sends a short command over the USB cable. The Arduino turns on one relay, and that opens the valve or pump for the zone.


6. Report (Pi)The Pi shows the live dashboard on your phone, raises alerts, and saves every action to a log file.




The rules the Pi follows


RuleWhy


Water a zone when its soil is below the crop's lower target, and stop at the upper targetEach crop has its own need: rice likes wet soil, wheat likes drier soil


Do not water if it is raining or rain is forecast at 60% or moreSaves water. The dashboard counts the litres saved


No watering from 11:00 to 16:00 unless the soil is very dryMidday heat evaporates water and can stress plants


Only one zone is watered at a time, driest firstProtects the power supply and keeps water pressure steady


Block the pumps when the tank is below 10%Stops the pump running dry, which damages it


A sensor stuck at 0 for 10 readings means a fault. Disable that zone and alertA broken sensor would otherwise water the zone forever


The Arduino turns the pump off by itself after 2 minutes without a new commandSafety if the Pi or the USB cable fails


How the controls on this page match the real parts


  • Base temperature slider: the LM35 temperature sensor. Hotter weather dries the soil faster.
  • Forecast slider: the weather data the Pi downloads.
  • Make it rain: the ESP32 rain sensor getting wet.
  • Crop dropdown: the crop profile you pick on the Pi dashboard.
  • Break zone 3 sensor: a soil sensor cable coming loose.
  • Manual pump: the button on the phone dashboard. It switches auto mode off.


Parts you need


HYBRID-X1 board (or an Arduino Uno, an ESP32 and a Raspberry Pi 4), 3 capacitive soil moisture sensors, 1 water level sensor, 1 LM35 temperature sensor, 1 rain sensor, a 3-channel relay module, 3 solenoid valves or small pumps (12 V), and a separate 12 V power supply for the valves.


Benefits


  • Saves water and electricity, because plants are watered only when needed.
  • No walking to the field: control and monitoring from a phone.
  • Warns about low tank and broken sensors before the crop suffers.
  • Keeps a log, so you can see how much each zone was watered.


This page is a simulation that runs in your browser. The code files in the Code tab are written for real hardware but have not been tested on real sensors, so calibrate the soil sensor values (DRY and WET in the Arduino file) and test each valve before leaving it unattended.
